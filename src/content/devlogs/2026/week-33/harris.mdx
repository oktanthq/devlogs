Every platform eventually admits its execution model. Week one of the log is the right time to admit ours — and to record how the team got there instead of just where it landed.

### The candidates

Three models were on the table:

- **Batch** — run everything in one big pass, synchronously. Simple to reason about, hard to make interactive.
- **Event-driven work graph** — work expressed as nodes and edges, flow signaled by events, execution supervised continuously.
- **Hybrid scheduling** — batches of work orchestrated by a generic scheduler.

### The decision

Oktant's workloads are pipelines whose steps produce and consume artifacts, and whose outcomes matter as much as their runtimes. That makes the event-driven work graph the natural shape: it renders analysis as three engineers can inspect it, degrades by node instead of by whole run, and leaves room for observability to hang off every edge.

Batch was tempting for its simplicity. It lost because Oktant's value is partly the *understood* run — a graph that can be analyzed, paused, and explained, not a void that occasionally returns output.

### What the choice commits us to

The decision commits early engineering to building a real runtime rather than wrapping subprocess calls. That is more work now and less wall-repair later. The log will record whether the trade was worth it the moment it stops being theoretical.
