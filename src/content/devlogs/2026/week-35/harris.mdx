Between the source a developer writes and the machine that executes it, there is a layer where Oktant actually gets to think. That layer is the internal representation, and this week it got a design.

### Why an IR exists

The IR is the answer to a simple problem: the parser output is shaped by how developers write, but the engine needs things shaped by how work executes. Rather than make either side compromise, there is a transformation boundary between them.

### What the IR must hold

- A **typed work graph** with explicit nodes and edges — no stringly-typed anything.
- **Data dependencies** stated as flow, separate from scheduling concerns.
- **Metadata** riding alongside: identity, provenance to source positions, and version.

### The discipline

The rules were written before the code: the IR is immutable once produced, transformations are functions that return new graphs rather than mutating, and every node knows its type. None of this is fashionable — it is how you keep an intermediate layer from rotting into a dumping ground.

With the IR specified, the core has both halves of its spine: the AST from Abdullah's parser in, the IR to the runtime out.
