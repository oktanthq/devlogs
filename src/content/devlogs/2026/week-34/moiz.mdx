Process isolation was decided early for Oktant because every downstream decision — security, resource accounting, restart semantics — hangs off it. This week locked the strategy in.

### The decision

Steps run with the narrowest privilege that still lets them do their job. No shared filesystem by default, no inherited environment, explicitly granted outputs only. The platform assumes steps are untrusted until a plan declares otherwise.

### Why strict

A platform that executes arbitrary work becomes trustworthy only at the isolation boundary. If resourced and contained, a broken step is an event; if not, it is an incident. The cost — more careful design at every seam — is one we accept now because retrofitting isolation is the most expensive refactor a platform can take.

### What it costs

Strict isolation makes the obvious path slightly harder: joining two steps now requires an explicit declaration between them. That friction is the point. It forces intentions into the plan, where they can be analyzed and audited, instead of leaving them implicit in the machine that happens to run.

The isolation story now exists in writing. The runtime work will earn the right to run on top of it.
